# Methylphenidate (Ritalin) - Comprehensive Pharmacological Profile
## Drug Interactions Focus for ADHD Treatment

**Research Date:** November 22, 2025
**Compiled by:** Deep Research Agent
**Confidence Rating System:** High = Multiple peer-reviewed sources + FDA label; Medium = 1-2 authoritative sources; Low = Limited or indirect evidence

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Methylphenidate is a central nervous system (CNS) stimulant primarily used to treat attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). Unlike many psychiatric medications, methylphenidate exhibits minimal cytochrome P450-mediated drug interactions because it is metabolized primarily by carboxylesterase 1 (CES1) rather than CYP enzymes. However, critical contraindications exist with MAOIs, and monitoring is required when combined with warfarin, anticonvulsants, and certain antidepressants. Sleep disturbances, particularly insomnia, are among the most common adverse effects, with meta-analytic evidence showing 2-3 fold increased risk compared to placebo.

## 1. MECHANISM OF ACTION AND PHARMACODYNAMICS

### Primary Mechanism
**Confidence: HIGH**

Methylphenidate functions as a norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor (NDRI) by blocking dopamine transporters (DAT) and norepinephrine transporters (NET) in presynaptic neurons, thereby increasing synaptic concentrations of these neurotransmitters primarily in the prefrontal cortex.

**Binding Affinity Data:**
- **In vivo human studies:** NET (ED50 = 0.14 mg/kg) > DAT (ED50 = 0.25 mg/kg)
- **Overall selectivity:** 2-3 fold selectivity for DAT over NET
- **In vitro correlation:** Affinities at DAT and NET are highly correlated (r² = 0.90)

**Sources:**
- [Unravelling the effects of methylphenidate on dopaminergic and noradrenergic circuits](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7360745/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Affinities of methylphenidate derivatives for transporters](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8786705/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Methylphenidate StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482451/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Secondary Receptor Activity
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

Methylphenidate acts as a weak agonist at the serotonin 1A receptor (5HT1A), which may contribute to elevated dopamine levels and therapeutic effects.

**Source:**
- [Methylphenidate StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482451/) - CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM

**Confidence: HIGH**

**Ritalin (Immediate Release):**
- Duration of action: 2-4 hours
- Half-life: 2.1 hours (adults), 2.4 hours (children)

- Initial peak at ~1 hour (22% immediate release overcoat)

- Biphasic profile: 50% IR, 50% ER
- First peak: 7 µg/L at 2.1 hours
- Second peak: 9.3 µg/L at 5.6 hours

### Metabolism
**Confidence: HIGH**

**Primary Metabolic Pathway:**
Methylphenidate undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism via hepatic carboxylesterase 1 (CES1A1) through de-esterification to ritalinic acid (RA), an inactive metabolite. This pathway is stereoselective, heavily favoring hydrolysis of the l-isomer while preserving the pharmacologically active d-isomer.

**Key Points:**
- **NOT metabolized by CYP450 enzymes** to any clinically relevant extent
- CYP inducers/inhibitors do not significantly affect methylphenidate pharmacokinetics
- Quinidine (potent CYP2D6 inhibitor) showed no effect on methylphenidate PK
- Methylphenidate does NOT inhibit CYP1A2, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A

**Minor Metabolic Pathways:**
- 6-oxo-methylphenidate (6-oxo-MPH) - up to 1.5% of dose
- p-hydroxy-methylphenidate (p-OH-MPH) - up to 2.5% of dose
- Both are inactive in humans

**Ethanol Interaction:**
When co-administered with alcohol, transesterification produces l-ethylphenidate, a novel active metabolite:
- Increases MPH AUC by 27% (105.2 vs 82.9 ng·hr/mL; P < 0.0001)
- Raises abuse potential concerns

**Sources:**
- [PharmGKB Summary - Methylphenidate Pathway](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6581573/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Methylphenidate Pathway Pharmacokinetics](https://www.clinpgx.org/pathway/PA166181002) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [FDA Ritalin LA Label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/021284s020lbl.pdf) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Pharmacogenetics - CES1 Gene Variations
**Confidence: HIGH**

**G143E Variant (rs71647871):**
Heterozygous carriers show dramatically altered pharmacokinetics:
- AUC: 208.7 vs 78.6 ng/mL·hrs (149% increase; P < 0.01)
- Cmax: 36 vs 13.8 ng/mL (P < 0.01)
- Half-life: 5.4 vs 3.0 hours (P < 0.01)
- Clinical impact: Required 29% lower dose (0.41 vs 0.57 mg/kg/day; P = 0.022)

**Copy Number Variations:**
- 4-copy CES1 genotype: 45-61% increased AUC (P ≤ 0.028)
- 3-copy genotype: Intermediate metabolism
- Paradoxically, high-copy individuals may have reduced metabolic activity due to downstream polymorphisms

**P-Glycoprotein (ABCB1):**
- ABCB1 c.2677G>T variant (rs2032582) associated with increased adverse reactions (OR: 9.04; P = 0.005)
- Minimal effect on brain MPH concentrations in knockout studies

**Source:**
- [PharmGKB Summary - Methylphenidate Pathway](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6581573/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Elimination
**Confidence: HIGH**

- **Urinary excretion:** 78-97% of dose within 48-96 hours
  - 60-86% as ritalinic acid
  - <2% unchanged methylphenidate
- **Fecal excretion:** 1-3% of dose
- **Systemic clearance:**
  - d-methylphenidate: 0.40 ± 0.12 L/h/kg
  - l-methylphenidate: 0.73 ± 0.28 L/h/kg

## 3. DRUG-DRUG INTERACTIONS

### Critical Interaction: CONTRAINDICATED

#### Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)
**Confidence: HIGH**

**Mechanism:** Combined use can cause hypertensive crisis due to excessive accumulation of norepinephrine and dopamine.

**Clinical Consequences:**
- Potential death, stroke, myocardial infarction
- Aortic dissection, renal failure

**Intervention:**
- **ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION**
- Avoid methylphenidate within 14 days of MAOI discontinuation
- MAOIs include: isocarboxazid, linezolid, methylene blue injection, phenelzine, tranylcypromine

**Emerging Evidence:**
Despite contraindication, some literature suggests combination may be safer than traditionally believed for methylphenidate (vs amphetamine). 50 years of use with no reported deaths, though this does not override FDA contraindication.

**Sources:**
- [DailyMed FDA Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0bf0835-6a2f-4067-a158-8b86c4b0668a) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Methylphenidate StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482451/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Combining Stimulants and MAOIs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4805402/) - CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM

---

### Major Interactions: Requires Monitoring

#### 1. Warfarin, Phenytoin, Tricyclic Antidepressants, SSRIs
**Confidence: HIGH**

**Mechanism:** Methylphenidate can inhibit the metabolism of these drugs, leading to increased plasma concentrations. The exact mechanism remains unclear, as methylphenidate is not a CYP substrate or inhibitor in vitro.

**Clinical Impact:**
- Increased drug levels and potential toxicity
- Phenytoin: Risk of toxicity (ataxia, nystagmus, cognitive impairment)
- Warfarin: Increased bleeding risk due to elevated INR
- TCAs/SSRIs: Enhanced therapeutic and adverse effects

**Intervention:**
- Monitor drug levels when initiating or discontinuing methylphenidate
- Adjust doses as needed
- For warfarin: More frequent INR monitoring

**Sources:**
- [Methylphenidate StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482451/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [GoodRx Methylphenidate Interactions](https://www.goodrx.com/methylphenidate/interactions) - CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM
- [Methylphenidate Under-recognized Drug Interactions](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281925840_Methylphenidate_and_Its_Under-recognized_Underexplained_and_Serious_Drug_Interactions_A_Review_of_the_Literature_with_Heightened_Concerns) - CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM

#### 2. Antihypertensive Medications
**Confidence: HIGH**

**Mechanism:** Methylphenidate's sympathomimetic effects antagonize antihypertensive action.

**Clinical Impact:**
- Reduced blood pressure control
- Mean BP increase: 2-4 mmHg systolic/diastolic
- Mean heart rate increase: 3-6 bpm
- Some patients may have larger increases

**Intervention:**
- Monitor blood pressure at each visit
- Adjust antihypertensive doses as needed

**Sources:**
- [DailyMed FDA Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0bf0835-6a2f-4067-a158-8b86c4b0668a) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Methylphenidate StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482451/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

#### 3. Halogenated Anesthetics
**Confidence: HIGH**

**Mechanism:** Combined use enhances risk of sudden heart rate and blood pressure increases during surgery.

**Intervention:**
- **Avoid methylphenidate on day of surgery** when halogenated anesthetics are planned

**Source:**
- [DailyMed FDA Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0bf0835-6a2f-4067-a158-8b86c4b0668a) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

#### 4. Risperidone
**Confidence: HIGH**

**Mechanism:** Unknown; possibly related to dopaminergic modulation.

**Clinical Impact:**
- Increased risk of extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS)
- Particularly during dose changes

**Intervention:**
- Monitor for EPS signs (tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia)
- Consider dose adjustments if EPS emerges

**Source:**
- [DailyMed FDA Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0bf0835-6a2f-4067-a158-8b86c4b0668a) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

#### 5. Alcohol (Ethanol)
**Confidence: HIGH**

**Mechanism:**
- Transesterification produces l-ethylphenidate (active metabolite)
- Methylphenidate's stimulant effects can mask alcohol's sedative effects

**Clinical Impact:**
- 27% increase in methylphenidate AUC
- Increased abuse potential
- Risk of severe respiratory depression (CNS depression masked)
- **Dose-dumping risk** with extended-release formulations (accelerated release)

**Intervention:**
- Advise patients to limit alcohol consumption
- Warn about masked intoxication

**Sources:**
- [PharmGKB Summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6581573/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Methylphenidate StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482451/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Moderate Interactions: Clinical Awareness

#### Serotonin Syndrome Risk
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

**Mechanism:** Methylphenidate has weak serotonin 1A agonist activity; theoretical risk when combined with serotonergic agents.

**Associated Medications:**
- SSRIs, SNRIs
- Tricyclic antidepressants
- MAOIs (contraindicated for other reasons)
- Opioids (tramadol, meperidine)
- Triptans

**Clinical Impact:**
- Risk of serotonin syndrome (agitation, hallucinations, fever, tachycardia, muscle rigidity, autonomic instability)
- Risk appears low in clinical practice but monitoring warranted

**Intervention:**
- Educate patients on serotonin syndrome symptoms
- Seek immediate medical attention if symptoms develop

**Sources:**
- [SingleCare Methylphenidate Interactions](https://www.singlecare.com/blog/methylphenidate-interactions/) - CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM
- [GoodRx Methylphenidate Interactions](https://www.goodrx.com/methylphenidate/interactions) - CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM

#### CNS Depressants and Benzodiazepines
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

**Mechanism:** Opposing pharmacodynamic effects; methylphenidate may mask sedation.

**Clinical Impact:**
- Methylphenidate's stimulant effects can mask CNS depression
- **Note:** Benzodiazepines are actually used therapeutically in methylphenidate overdose/toxicity

**Intervention:**
- Monitor for efficacy of both agents
- Caution patients about unpredictable effects

**Sources:**
- [Healthline Ritalin Interactions](https://www.healthline.com/health/drugs/ritalin-interactions) - CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM
- [Methylphenidate StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482451/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Important Note: CYP Enzyme Interactions
**Confidence: HIGH**

**Methylphenidate is:**
- **NOT a CYP450 substrate** (not metabolized by CYP enzymes)
- **NOT a CYP450 inhibitor** (does not inhibit CYP1A2, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A)
- **NOT a CYP450 inducer**

**Clinical Significance:**
- CYP-mediated drug interactions are NOT expected
- Can be safely combined with many medications metabolized by CYP enzymes
- Exceptions: Warfarin, phenytoin, TCAs, SSRIs (mechanism unclear, possibly non-CYP metabolic inhibition)

**Sources:**
- [PharmGKB Summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6581573/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Single-dose PK in CYP2D6 metabolizers](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10831022/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

## 4. EFFECTS ON SLEEP AND INSOMNIA

### Prevalence of Sleep Disturbances
**Confidence: HIGH**

#### Meta-Analysis Evidence (35 RCTs, 5,685 patients)

**Overall Sleep-Related Adverse Events:**
- **Insomnia (general):** RR = 1.61 (P < 0.001)
- **Initial insomnia (sleep onset):** RR = 2.78 (P < 0.001)
- **Middle insomnia (nighttime awakenings):** RR = 2.97 (P < 0.001)
- **Combined insomnia:** RR = 2.97 (P < 0.001)
- **Sleep disorder:** RR = 1.99 (P < 0.001)

**Formulation Differences:**
- Extended-release: Mean RR = 4.8
- Immediate-release: Mean RR = 1.9
- **Statistical significance:** P = 0.03

**Dosing Schedule Impact:**
- Once-daily: Mean RR = 4.6
- Twice-daily: Mean RR = 1.7
- Three-times-daily: Mean RR = 2.5

**Source:**
- [Meta-Analysis: Sleep-Associated AEs with Methylphenidate](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8074661/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

#### Individual Study Data

**Children and Adolescents:**
- **25-47.2%** reported insomnia in observational studies
- **20%** medication-related insomnia in controlled trials
- **31.1%** difficulty falling asleep (95% CI: 26.5-36.2%; 82 studies, 11,507 participants)
- **17.9%** sleep problems in non-comparative cohort studies

**Adults:**
- **60-80%** of adults with ADHD report sleep problems (baseline)
- Effects of stimulant treatment are complex and varied
- Some patients experience worsening; others report improvement

**Sources:**
- [Cochrane Review: Methylphenidate Adverse Events](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6494554/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Adverse reactions in referral center](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4262859/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Sleep in Adults with ADHD](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2276739/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Clinical Implications
**Confidence: HIGH**

**Assessment Recommendations:**
Clinicians should specifically explore:
- **Initial insomnia:** Difficulty falling asleep
- **Middle insomnia:** Nighttime awakenings
- **Terminal insomnia:** Early morning awakening
- **Sleep quality:** Restorative value of sleep
- **Combined insomnia:** Multiple subtypes

Rather than simply asking "Do you have sleep problems?"

**Placebo Effect Constraint:**
High correlation between placebo and drug adverse event rates (r = 0.89) suggests observed relative risks are substantially predicted by placebo baseline rates.

**Source:**
- [Meta-Analysis: Sleep-Associated AEs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8074661/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

## 5. CONTRAINDICATIONS AND WARNINGS

### Absolute Contraindications
**Confidence: HIGH**

1. **Known hypersensitivity** to methylphenidate or product components
2. **Current MAOI use** or within 14 days of MAOI discontinuation
3. **Glaucoma** (angle-closure risk)
4. **Motor tics** or family history/diagnosis of **Tourette syndrome**
5. **Severe anxiety, tension, or agitation** (drug may aggravate symptoms)
6. **Children under 6 years of age**

**Sources:**
- [DailyMed FDA Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0bf0835-6a2f-4067-a158-8b86c4b0668a) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [FDA Ritalin Label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2019/010187s071s082,018029s041s051lbl.pdf) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Black Box Warning: Abuse and Dependence
**Confidence: HIGH**

Methylphenidate is a **Schedule II controlled substance (C-II)** with high potential for abuse and misuse, which can lead to substance use disorder and addiction.

**Risk Factors:**
- History of substance abuse
- Intravenous abuse potential (can cause digital ulceration)
- Diversion for non-medical use

**Source:**
- [DailyMed FDA Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0bf0835-6a2f-4067-a158-8b86c4b0668a) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Serious Cardiovascular Warnings
**Confidence: HIGH**

#### Sudden Death and Serious Cardiac Events

**Pediatric Patients:**
Sudden death reported in children and adolescents with:
- Structural cardiac abnormalities
- Cardiomyopathy
- Serious arrhythmias
- Coronary artery disease

**Adult Patients:**
- Sudden death
- Stroke
- Myocardial infarction
- Reported at usual ADHD doses

**Pre-treatment Screening:**
- Assess for structural cardiac abnormalities
- Obtain family history of sudden death or ventricular arrhythmia
- Consider ECG if concerning history

**Avoid Use In:**
- Known serious structural cardiac abnormalities
- Cardiomyopathy
- Serious cardiac arrhythmia
- Coronary artery disease
- Other serious cardiac disease

#### Blood Pressure and Heart Rate Effects

**Expected Changes:**
- BP increase: 2-4 mmHg (mean)
- Heart rate increase: 3-6 bpm (mean)
- Some patients may have larger increases

**Monitoring:**
- Measure BP and HR at each visit
- Consider dose reduction or discontinuation if clinically significant increases

**Source:**
- [DailyMed FDA Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0bf0835-6a2f-4067-a158-8b86c4b0668a) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Psychiatric Warnings
**Confidence: HIGH**

#### Pre-existing Psychosis
May exacerbate symptoms of behavior disturbance and thought disorder.

#### Bipolar Disorder
Screen for risk factors before initiating treatment:
- Family history of bipolar disorder, depression, or suicide
- Mixed/manic episodes may be precipitated

#### New Psychotic or Manic Symptoms
May occur even without prior psychiatric history:
- Hallucinations, delusional thinking
- Mania
- Incidence: ~0.1% in stimulant-treated patients vs 0% placebo

**Intervention:**
Consider discontinuation if symptoms emerge.

**Source:**
- [DailyMed FDA Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0bf0835-6a2f-4067-a158-8b86c4b0668a) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Other Serious Warnings

#### Peripheral Vasculopathy / Raynaud's Phenomenon
**Confidence: HIGH**

- Digital numbness, color changes, temperature changes
- May progress to digital ulceration or soft tissue breakdown
- Monitor for signs; consider dose reduction or discontinuation

#### Priapism
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

- Prolonged, painful erections (>4 hours)
- May require surgical intervention
- Can occur with or without sexual stimulation
- Immediate medical attention required

#### Growth Suppression (Pediatric)
**Confidence: HIGH**

- Temporary slowing of growth rate
- 7 days/week dosing throughout year:
  - ~2 cm less growth in height over 3 years
  - ~2.7 kg less weight gain over 3 years
- Monitor height and weight during treatment

**Sources:**
- [DailyMed FDA Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0bf0835-6a2f-4067-a158-8b86c4b0668a) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH
- [Methylphenidate StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482451/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

### Seizure Risk
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

- May lower seizure threshold
- Use caution in patients with history of seizures or EEG abnormalities
- Discontinue if seizures occur

**Source:**
- [Methylphenidate interactions](https://www.singlecare.com/blog/methylphenidate-interactions/) - CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM

## 7. MONITORING PARAMETERS

### Required Monitoring
**Confidence: HIGH**

**At Each Visit:**
- Blood pressure
- Heart rate
- Signs/symptoms of cardiac disease (chest pain, syncope)
- Psychiatric symptoms (mood changes, psychotic symptoms, aggression)
- Appetite and weight
- Sleep quality
- Efficacy assessment

**Periodic Monitoring:**
- Height and weight (pediatric patients) - plot on growth curves
- Complete blood count (CBC)
- Signs of abuse or dependence
- Digital perfusion (check for Raynaud's)

**When Applicable:**
- Drug levels: Warfarin (INR), phenytoin, antidepressants if co-prescribed
- ECG: If concerning cardiac history
- Intraocular pressure: If glaucoma risk

**Sources:**

## 8. SPECIAL POPULATIONS

### Pregnancy (Category C)
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

- Results inconclusive; animal studies show some adverse effects
- Use only if benefit outweighs risk
- No adequate well-controlled studies in pregnant women

### Lactation
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

- Excreted in breast milk
- Maintain relative infant dose below 10%
- Monitor infant for adverse effects (poor feeding, agitation, poor weight gain)

### Pediatric Use
**Confidence: HIGH**

- **Approved for ages ≥6 years**
- Not recommended for children <6 years (safety and efficacy not established)
- Monitor growth parameters closely

### Geriatric Use
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

- Limited data
- Increased cardiovascular risk
- Start with lower doses; titrate carefully

## 9. OVERDOSE AND TOXICOLOGY

### Toxic Doses
**Confidence: HIGH**

- **Immediate-release:** >60 mg
- **Extended-release:** >120 mg

### Symptoms of Overdose
**Confidence: HIGH**

**CNS:**
- Tremors, hyperreflexia
- Convulsions
- Euphoria, confusion
- Hallucinations, delirium
- Agitation, restlessness

**Cardiovascular:**
- Tachycardia, palpitations
- Cardiac arrhythmias
- Hypertension or hypotension

**Other:**
- Flushing, fever (hyperpyrexia)
- Mydriasis (dilated pupils)
- Dry mucous membranes
- Rhabdomyolysis

### Management
**Confidence: HIGH**

1. **Supportive care:**
   - Oxygen, IV fluids
   - Cooling for hyperthermia
   - Continuous monitoring

2. **Benzodiazepines for:**
   - Dystonia
   - Agitation
   - Convulsions

3. **Contact poison control immediately**

4. **Do NOT use activated charcoal routinely** (consult poison control)

**Source:**
- [Methylphenidate StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482451/) - CONFIDENCE: HIGH

## 11. CLINICAL DECISION SUPPORT: DRUG INTERACTION CHECKLIST

### Before Prescribing Methylphenidate, Screen for:

**CONTRAINDICATED (Do Not Use):**
- [ ] Current MAOI use or within 14 days
- [ ] Glaucoma
- [ ] Severe anxiety/agitation
- [ ] Motor tics or Tourette syndrome

**REQUIRES CLOSE MONITORING:**
- [ ] Warfarin (monitor INR more frequently)
- [ ] Phenytoin or other anticonvulsants (monitor drug levels)
- [ ] Tricyclic antidepressants (monitor for increased effects)
- [ ] SSRIs (monitor for serotonin syndrome, though risk low)
- [ ] Antihypertensives (monitor BP at each visit)
- [ ] Risperidone (monitor for extrapyramidal symptoms)
- [ ] Alcohol use (counsel on risks, dose-dumping)

**SURGICAL PLANNING:**
- [ ] Discontinue day of surgery if halogenated anesthetics planned

**CARDIOVASCULAR SCREENING:**
- [ ] Structural heart disease
- [ ] Arrhythmias
- [ ] Family history of sudden cardiac death
- [ ] Hypertension (uncontrolled)

**PSYCHIATRIC SCREENING:**
- [ ] History of psychosis
- [ ] Bipolar disorder or family history
- [ ] Suicidal ideation/attempts
- [ ] Severe anxiety or agitation
