## TABLE 1: Sleep Medication Evidence Quality in ADHD

| Medication | Evidence Grade | Efficacy in ADHD | Safety Profile | Recommendation |
|------------|----------------|------------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Melatonin** | A-B | EFFECTIVE<br>Sleep onset: -27 min<br>(4 RCTs) | Safe long-term<br>Adverse events: headache, mood changes (low incidence) | **FIRST-LINE**<br>3-6 mg at bedtime |
| **Clonidine** | B-C | EFFECTIVE<br>Improves sleep latency, quality<br>(Retrospective data) | Monitor BP/HR<br>Somnolence 15-20% with stimulants<br>Rebound HTN if stopped suddenly | **SECOND-LINE**<br>0.05-0.1 mg at bedtime |
| **L-Theanine** | C | EFFECTIVE<br>(1 RCT positive) | Well-tolerated<br>Mild-moderate AEs | **ALTERNATIVE**<br>Limited data |
| **Guanfacine** | C | **NOT EFFECTIVE**<br>Failed vs placebo<br>Reduced REM/total sleep | Generally safe with stimulants<br>FDA-approved combination for ADHD | **NOT for sleep**<br>ADHD treatment only |
| **Trazodone** | D | **UNKNOWN**<br>No RCTs in pediatric ADHD<br>Widely used off-label (8.4% prevalence) | Long-term safety NOT established<br>Contraindicated in bipolar<br>3x risk self-harm with SSRIs | **WEAK** off-label<br>No RCT support |
| **Mirtazapine** | D | **UNKNOWN**<br>Limited autism data<br>55% perceived effective in depression | No pediatric ADHD safety data | Insufficient evidence |
| **Gabapentin** | D | Possible benefit<br>(1 case series: 78% response) | Agitated awakenings reported | Insufficient evidence |
| **Zolpidem** | C | **NOT EFFECTIVE**<br>Failed vs placebo in ADHD RCT | Neuropsychiatric adverse effects<br>Never FDA-approved for insomnia | **AVOID**<br>No efficacy + safety concerns |
| **Eszopiclone** | C | **NOT EFFECTIVE**<br>Failed vs placebo in ADHD RCT | Well-tolerated but no benefit | **AVOID**<br>No efficacy |
| **Benzodiazepines** | D | No pediatric ADHD efficacy data | Dependence, tolerance, cognitive impairment<br>Suicide risk after discontinuation | **AVOID**<br>Safety concerns |
| **DORAs (all)** | E | **UNKNOWN**<br>Zero ADHD studies | Unknown in ADHD<br>Safe in other psychiatric conditions | **CANNOT RECOMMEND**<br>Evidence gap |

**Evidence Grading:**
- **A**: High-quality RCTs, systematic reviews, consistent findings
- **B**: RCTs with limitations, cohort studies
- **C**: Case-control, retrospective, inconsistent RCT results
- **D**: Case series, expert opinion, extrapolated data
- **E**: Evidence gap - no published studies

## TABLE 2: ADHD Medication Effects on Sleep

| ADHD Medication | Sleep Onset Latency Change | Other Sleep Effects | Evidence Quality | Clinical Implication |
|-----------------|----------------------------|---------------------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Methylphenidate** | **+39.2 minutes** | Worsens sleep quality<br>Harder to wake up<br>Longer to fall asleep | A (RCT, n=85) | Highest sleep disruption<br>Consider timing, dose, formulation |
| **Amphetamines** | Similar to MPH (extrapolated) | Dose-dependent insomnia | B | Earlier morning dosing<br>Lower doses if possible |
| **Atomoxetine** | **+12.1 minutes**<br>(significantly less than MPH, p<0.001) | More night awakenings<br>Better subjective sleep quality<br>Somnolence early in treatment | A (RCT, n=85) | **Best sleep profile**<br>Consider switch if stimulant insomnia severe |
| **Guanfacine ER** | No improvement vs placebo | Reduced REM sleep<br>Reduced total sleep time | C (RCT negative) | Does NOT help sleep<br>Use for ADHD, not insomnia |
| **Clonidine** | Improves sleep onset | Sedating effect<br>Improves sleep latency and quality | B-C | Can help stimulant-induced insomnia<br>Monitor BP/HR |

**Key Finding:** Atomoxetine increases sleep onset latency by 27 minutes LESS than methylphenidate (12 min vs 39 min, p<0.001)

**Source:** Sangal et al., Sleep 2006;29(12):1573-1585 (PMID: 17252888)

## TABLE 3: Safety of ADHD Medication + Sleep Aid Combinations

| Combination | Evidence Grade | Safety Status | Drug Interaction | Monitoring Required | Recommendation |
|-------------|----------------|---------------|------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Stimulants + Melatonin** | A | **SAFE**<br>Multiple RCTs | None | Sleep efficacy<br>ADHD symptom control | **FIRST-LINE**<br>Safe, effective |
| **Stimulants + Guanfacine ER** | A | **SAFE**<br>FDA-approved | None (approved combination) | BP, HR, somnolence | **SAFE**<br>For ADHD, not sleep |
| **Stimulants + Clonidine** | B | **Generally SAFE** | None significant | BP (orthostatic hypotension)<br>HR<br>Sedation<br>Taper slowly | **REASONABLE**<br>Monitor cardiovascular |
| **Atomoxetine + Trazodone** | B | **SAFE**<br>No PK interaction | AUC ratio 1.01-1.06<br>(no clinically significant interaction) | Sleep efficacy<br>Mood (trazodone concerns) | **SAFE to combine**<br>No dose adjustment needed |
| **Atomoxetine + Melatonin** | A | **SAFE** (extrapolated) | None expected | Sleep efficacy | **SAFE**<br>No interaction expected |
| **Stimulants + Trazodone** | D | Likely safe but limited data | None known | Mood (bipolar risk)<br>Sleep efficacy | **CAUTION**<br>Off-label, limited data |
| **Stimulants + DORAs** | E | **UNKNOWN** | Theoretical: Low risk<br>(different mechanisms, minimal CYP overlap)<br>**Clinical data: NONE** | If attempted:<br>- Sleep efficacy<br>- ADHD symptoms<br>- Mood (depression/SI)<br>- Next-day alertness | **CANNOT RECOMMEND**<br>No published data |
| **Stimulants + Benzodiazepines** | D | **CAUTION** | None pharmacokinetic<br>Risk: polypharmacy overdose | Dependence<br>Cognitive function<br>Respiratory depression | **AVOID**<br>No efficacy + safety concerns |
| **Stimulants + Z-drugs** | C-D | **CAUTION** | None significant | Cognitive function<br>Next-day effects | **AVOID**<br>Failed efficacy trials |
| **Trazodone + SSRIs** | C | **RISKY** | mCPP accumulation<br>3x increased self-harm risk<br>Reduced antidepressant effect | Mood<br>Suicidality | **AVOID combination**<br>Significant interaction |

**Key Safety Findings:**
1. **Safest combinations:** Stimulants + melatonin (Grade A evidence)
2. **FDA-approved:** Stimulants + guanfacine ER (for ADHD, not sleep)
3. **Unknown:** ALL DORA + ADHD medication combinations (zero published data)
4. **Risky:** Trazodone + SSRIs (3x self-harm risk)

## TABLE 4: DORA Pharmacology Comparison

| Parameter | Suvorexant (Belsomra) | Lemborexant (Dayvigo) | Daridorexant (Quviviq) |
|-----------|----------------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **FDA Approval** | August 2014 | December 2019 | January 2022 |
| **Adult Dosing** | 10-20 mg | 5-10 mg | 25-50 mg |
| **Elimination Half-life** | ~12 hours | ~18-55 hours | ~8 hours |
| **Clearance** | 4.81 L/h | 22.7 L/h | 2.4 L/h (at 500 ng/ml) |
| **Metabolism** | CYP3A4 | CYP3A4 | CYP3A4 |
| **Next-Day Effects** | Possible carryover | Longer duration<br>More likely | **Minimal**<br>Shortest half-life |
| **Depression Risk** | **Dose-dependent worsening** reported<br>Suicidal ideation in some studies | Less data on mood effects | **More likely to cause depression** reported |
| **Pediatric Approval** | ❌ No | ❌ No | ❌ No |
| **ADHD Studies** | ❌ None | ❌ None | ❌ None |
| **Best For** | Standard insomnia | Longer sleep need | **Minimal hangover**<br>Need morning alertness |

**Drug Interaction Summary:**
- **CYP3A4 strong inhibitors** (ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin): DECREASE DORA dose
- **CYP3A4 strong inducers** (rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin): INCREASE DORA dose
- **ADHD stimulants**: NOT significant CYP3A4 substrates → Low theoretical interaction risk
- **Atomoxetine**: Minimal CYP3A4 inhibition → Low theoretical interaction risk

**Clinical Implication:** If DORAs were to be used in ADHD (off-label, lacking evidence), daridorexant may be preferable due to shortest half-life and minimal next-day effects.

## TABLE 5: Clinical Practice Guidelines Summary

| Organization | Guideline | Year | Key Recommendations for ADHD Insomnia |
|--------------|-----------|------|--------------------------------------|
| **American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM)** | Pediatric Insomnia Consensus | 2005/2006 | • Medication NOT first-line<br>• Use with behavioral interventions<br>• No FDA-approved pediatric sleep meds<br>• Diagnostically driven approach |
| **AASM** | Adult Pharmacologic Treatment | 2017 | • Chronic insomnia focus<br>• Does NOT address ADHD specifically |
| **AASM** | Behavioral Treatments Update | 2022 | • CBT-I as gold standard<br>• Limited pediatric validation |
| **American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP)** | ADHD Diagnosis/Treatment | 2019 | • Evaluate baseline sleep patterns<br>• Sleep hygiene first<br>• Behavioral interventions<br>• **NO specific pharmacotherapy recommendations** |
| **American Psychiatric Association (APA)** | ADHD Treatment Guidelines | **NOT PUBLISHED** | • No comprehensive ADHD treatment guidelines exist<br>• Adult guidelines in development (expected 2025) |
| **International Pediatric Sleep Association** | Melatonin in Developmental Disorders | Ongoing | • Endorses 2-10 mg melatonin<br>• For developmentally disabled children age 2+ |

**Guideline Gap:** NO evidence-based pharmacotherapy guidelines exist specifically for managing ADHD-related insomnia

**Current Standard of Care (consensus-based):**
1. Sleep hygiene and behavioral interventions
2. ADHD medication optimization
3. Melatonin if above fail
4. Off-label options (clonidine, trazodone) with limited evidence

## TABLE 7: Melatonin in ADHD - Detailed Evidence

| Study | Design | Sample | Intervention | Sleep Outcome | ADHD Outcome | Adverse Events |
|-------|--------|--------|--------------|---------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Van der Heijden 2007** | RCT, DB, PC | 105 med-free children<br>Ages 6-12 | 3 or 6 mg<br>4 weeks | Sleep onset:<br>**-26.9 ± 47.8 min** (melatonin)<br>vs +10.5 ± 37.4 min (placebo)<br>**p < 0.0001** | No effect on behavior, cognition, QOL | Not specified |
| **Weiss 2006** | RCT, DB, PC, crossover | 27 stimulant-treated<br>Ages 6-14 | Sleep hygiene +<br>5 mg melatonin<br>30 days | Safe and effective<br>(specific metrics not in abstract) | **ADHD benefits maintained** | Safe |
| **Hoebert 2009** | Long-term follow-up | Children with ADHD and chronic SOI | Melatonin (dose varied) | **88% effective long-term** | N/A | **No serious AEs** |
| **Mohammadi 2012** | RCT | 50 children (ASD+ADHD)<br>Ages 7-12 | 3-6 mg<br>8 weeks | Total sleep:<br>8.0→8.5 hr (melatonin)<br>8.8→8.3 hr (control)<br>**p = 0.19 (NS)** | N/A | Fatigue 18.9%<br>Vomiting 16.8%<br>Mood swings 13.7% |

**Summary:**
- **Best evidence:** Van der Heijden 2007 (largest RCT, significant effect)
- **Safety:** Long-term use up to 2 years without serious adverse events
- **Limitation:** Mohammadi study (ASD+ADHD) showed NO significant benefit - only ADHD-specific study with negative findings
- **Clinical use:** Despite mixed evidence, melatonin remains best-studied and safest option

**Full Citations:**
- Van der Heijden et al., J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry 2007;46(2):233-241 (PMID: 17242627)
- Weiss et al., J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry 2006;45(5):512-519 (PMID: 16670647)
- Hoebert et al., J Pineal Res 2009;47(1):1-7
- Mohammadi et al., referenced in systematic review PMC7325410

## QUICK DECISION ALGORITHM

### Patient: Child/Adolescent with ADHD + Insomnia

```
START → Is insomnia new or worsened after starting ADHD med?
    ↓ YES
    → Step 1: OPTIMIZE ADHD MEDICATION
       • Earliest morning dose
       • Consider shorter-acting formulation
       • Evaluate if dose can be reduced
       • Consider atomoxetine if severe
    ↓
    → Step 2: BEHAVIORAL INTERVENTIONS (4-8 weeks)
       • Sleep hygiene education
       • Consistent bedtime routines
       • Screen time limits
       ↓ Failed
    → Step 3: MELATONIN TRIAL (4-8 weeks)
       • 3 mg initially
       • Increase to 6 mg if needed
       • 30-60 min before bedtime
       ↓ Failed
    → Step 4: CLONIDINE (if appropriate)
       • 0.05-0.1 mg at bedtime
       • Monitor BP, HR
       ↓ Failed
    → Step 5: SPECIALIST REFERRAL
       • Sleep medicine
       • Child psychiatry
       • Consider polysomnography to rule out primary sleep disorder
```

### Should I use a DORA?

```
NO published data exists
    ↓
Have you tried:
    • ADHD med optimization? → If NO, try first
    • Sleep hygiene? → If NO, try first
    • Melatonin? → If NO, try first
    • Clonidine? → If NO, consider
    ↓ ALL tried and failed
Consider:
    • Informed consent about lack of ADHD data
    • Close monitoring (sleep, mood, ADHD symptoms, alertness)
    • Daridorexant may be preferable (shortest half-life)
    • Low theoretical drug interaction risk but UNKNOWN clinical safety
```
